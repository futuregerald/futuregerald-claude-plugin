# Meeting Analysis Questions

Work through these questions **before** writing the debrief. These are your internal analysis steps — don't output the answers directly, but use them to inform every section of the final debrief.

Strong models: treat as a checklist. Weaker models: answer each question explicitly in your thinking, then synthesize into the template.

---

## Pass 1: Comprehension (What happened?)

Read the full transcript and answer:

1. **Who was in the meeting?** List every speaker. Note who spoke the most vs. the least.
2. **What were the distinct topics discussed?** List each topic as a 3-5 word label. A new topic starts when the subject changes, not when a new person speaks.
3. **For each topic:** What position did each speaker take? Did they agree, disagree, defer, or stay silent?
4. **What information was shared?** List any facts, updates, numbers, names, dates, or status changes that were communicated.
5. **What was explicitly decided?** Only include things where someone said "let's do X" or "we'll go with Y" and the other party agreed.
6. **What was explicitly asked for?** Any requests, favors, asks — whether or not they were agreed to.
7. **What time-sensitive items were mentioned?** Deadlines, upcoming events, windows of opportunity.

---

## Pass 2: Relationships & Dynamics (Who has power? Who's aligned?)

8. **Who set the agenda?** Who brought the topics? Who controlled topic transitions?
9. **Who asked questions vs. who provided answers?** The question-asker often holds less information but may hold more authority.
10. **Were there moments of agreement?** What specifically did they agree on? Was the agreement enthusiastic or reluctant?
11. **Were there moments of tension or disagreement?** Even subtle ones — hedging, redirecting, "yeah but..." patterns.
12. **Did anyone make a concession?** Changed their position or dropped a point? Why?
13. **Did anyone deflect or avoid a topic?** What topic, and what might that mean?
14. **How did the conversation end?** On a high note, with unresolved tension, rushed, or with clear next steps?
15. **Power dynamics:** Based on language patterns, who appears to be the more senior / more influential person? Does the conversation flow match that hierarchy, or is someone punching above/below their weight?

---

## Pass 3: Commitments & Accountability (Who promised what?)

16. **List every commitment made in this meeting.** A commitment is when someone says they will do something specific. Include:
    - Who made it
    - What exactly they committed to
    - Whether a timeline was given
    - How strong the language was ("I'll definitely..." vs. "we should probably..." vs. "let me look into...")
17. **Rate each commitment's confidence:**
    - Strong language + specific action + timeline = High
    - Acknowledged but vague or no timeline = Medium
    - Deflected, hedged, or "we should" without "I will" = Low
18. **From past meetings (if available):** Were previous commitments fulfilled, mentioned, or silently dropped?
19. **Are there commitments that sound good but lack an owner?** "We need to..." without "I will..." is not a real commitment.

---

## Pass 4: Subtext & What Wasn't Said (The second brain layer)

20. **What topics from previous meetings were NOT brought up?** Are any of those still relevant?
21. **Did anyone share information that seemed politically sensitive or carefully framed?** What were they really communicating?
22. **Were there moments someone started to say something and pulled back?** Or changed direction mid-sentence?
23. **What assumptions are both parties operating under?** Are any of those assumptions potentially wrong?
24. **Is anyone protecting someone or something?** Buffering bad news, shielding a team member, managing up?
25. **What would a cynical interpretation of this conversation look like?** (Not to present as truth, but to stress-test the optimistic read.)
26. **What would this conversation look like to someone outside the organization?** What's obvious to an outsider that insiders might miss?

---

## Pass 5: Risks & Recommendations (So what? Now what?)

27. **What could go wrong based on what was discussed?** Think about:
    - Commitments that might not be fulfilled (low-confidence items)
    - Organizational changes that could create confusion
    - Information gaps that could lead to mistakes
    - Dependencies on people who aren't in the room
28. **What should the user do in the next 48 hours?** Anything time-sensitive?
29. **What should the user do before the next meeting with this person?** Preparation, follow-ups, pre-reads?
30. **Is there a conversation the user should have with someone else based on this meeting?** Who and about what?
31. **What's the single most important takeaway?** If the user remembers only one thing from this meeting, what should it be?
32. **Draft a follow-up message if appropriate.** Would a short Slack message or email reinforce a key point, lock in a commitment, or move something forward?

---

## Pass 6: Patterns (Only if past meeting history is available)

33. **Are there recurring themes across meetings with this person?** Topics that keep coming up?
34. **Is the relationship trending positively, negatively, or flat?** Compare the tone, openness, and productivity across meetings.
35. **Are there chronic open items?** Things discussed repeatedly but never resolved?
36. **Has the balance of the conversation shifted?** Is one person driving more/less than before?
37. **Are commitments being honored at a higher or lower rate over time?**

---

## Synthesis Checklist

Before writing the final debrief, verify:

- [ ] Every topic from the transcript is accounted for (either in a section or deliberately omitted with reason)
- [ ] Every commitment has an owner and confidence rating
- [ ] The TL;DR captures the 3-5 most important points — not just the first 3 topics discussed
- [ ] "My read" sections contain genuine analysis, not restated summaries
- [ ] Recommendations are specific and actionable (who, what, by when)
- [ ] Past meeting commitments were cross-referenced if history was available
- [ ] Risks cite specific evidence from the transcript
- [ ] Follow-up fuel is something the user could actually send or say
